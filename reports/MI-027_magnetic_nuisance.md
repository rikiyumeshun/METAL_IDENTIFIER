# MI-027 — Magnetic signatures and microstructure nuisance
## Evidence
Wang & Li (2025), https://doi.org/10.1177/13835416241297465 : five carbon levels 0.069, 0.14, 0.51, 0.54, 0.68 wt%, five parallel specimens per level and five repeated sets. Reported MBN envelope peak vs carbon R²=.9915; coercivity increased and measured saturation induction decreased in their tests. Their setup uses a magnetic yoke, excitation coil, Hall sensor, induction coil and high-frequency pickup, with a 40–120 kHz filter and 10 Hz excitation selected for their apparatus. This is not a demonstrated S45C/S50C classifier or a cheap instrument. Review https://doi.org/10.3390/cryst16020149 and https://doi.org/10.3390/s26010258 discuss stress, phase, texture and hardness confounders. SAE1040/4140 comparison https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1044580306002026 shows dependence on microstructure. Candidate features: peak field, peak amplitude, RMS, spectral band ratios, coercivity, differential permeability, remanence, hysteresis area. Do not assume independent information.
## Carbon-only limit
S45C .42-.48 and S50C .47-.53 wt% C (https://www.chubukohan.co.jp/product/jis/product06/). Equal priors and uniform within each range imply perfect carbon measurement yields Bayes accuracy 1-.5*.01/.06=91.6667%; reject common interval yields 83.3333% coverage and perfect accepted accuracy under this ideal binary model. Real production distribution and grade provenance unknown; overlap is real, numeric ceiling depends on assumed distribution, not universal.
## Simulation
40,000 synthetic specimens reused across 96 conditions (3,840,000 condition-method trials). Carbon uniformly sampled by grade. All errors in carbon-equivalent wt percentage points, NOT measured magnetic voltage or field. Shared heat-treatment/stress nuisance is zero-mean Gaussian SD 0/.025/.05/.10; independent readout noise SD .005/.02/.05. n=1/4/16 total readings. Single channel repeats leave nuisance unchanged. Proxy observes nuisance with .02 SD and uses conditional Gaussian shrinkage (additional measurement not included in n). Opposite channel is an ideal design target: equally split even n across channels with matched carbon sensitivity and exactly opposite nuisance sensitivity, yielding cancellation; no evidence that real magnetic features achieve this. Scores use midpoint .475 and uniform-convolved Gaussian likelihood for posterior >=.9; no unknown classes. In .05 nuisance/.02 sensor SD case, 4 readings single 67.93%, proxy 81.64%, ideal opposite 88.59%; 16 readings 68.26%,82.93%,91.09%. Four-readings coverage at nominal posterior >=.9: 3.13%,40.06%,63.74%, accepted accuracy 91.77%,96.53%,98.67%. These are hypothetical sensitivity analyses, not physical calibration.
## Practical plan
Measure MBN and hysteresis simultaneously; start with documented coupons and independent hardness, heat-treatment and surface metadata. Acquire multiple magnetizing directions and frequencies to estimate nuisance sensitivity and grade effect; characterize sign and magnitude with held-out lots. High frequency pickup requires appropriate low-noise frontend/anti-aliasing or analog envelope detection, not the slow MI-023 ADC directly. Screen 40–120 kHz only as literature-informed starting band. Include demagnetization history, yoke contact, temperature, residual stress, grain orientation and magnetic safety/fixture controls. SCM440 has alloying contrasts (Cr/Mo, see https://www.sheng-maw.com.tw/aboutus.php?id=16); prioritize element-selective verification alongside magnetics. Do not claim exact grade if specifications overlap; provenance may be required.
