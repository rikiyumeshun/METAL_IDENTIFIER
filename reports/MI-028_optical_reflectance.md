# MI-028 — Optical reflectance
## Data
Public-domain CC0 refractiveindex.info database, Johnson & Christy 1972 (Cu, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.6.4370), Johnson & Christy 1974 (Fe, Ni, Ti, Cr, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.9.5056), Rakic 1995 (Al, DOI 10.1364/AO.34.004755). Raw nk data fetched from source GitHub repository and linearly interpolated at 450,525,630,850,940 nm. Normal-incidence Fresnel reflectance R=((n-1)^2+k^2)/((n+1)^2+k^2). Pure-element optical constants, not engineering alloy spectra or guaranteed bulk surface values. Source URLs recorded per row. Relevant ambient reflectance research https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jap/article/124/10/105107/155575/Reflectance-determination-of-optical-spectral (surface finish effects, Al absorption around 840 nm). Prior LED illumination material classification https://www.gujinwei.org/research/fisherlight/ ; chemical color sorting https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/alloy-selective-optical-sorting-mixed-post-consumer-aluminum-scrap-streams .
## Simulation
6 pure elements, 1000 draws each, same 6000 samples reused across 54 conditions (324000 condition-method trials). Three or five ideal narrowband samples. Signal y=g*R*exp(b*(lambda-650)/250)+noise, g uniform .35–1; spectral slope b Gaussian SD 0/.05/.15; additive normalized readout SD .005/.02/.05. This is a sensitivity stress model, not measured surface roughness/oxide data. Methods raw nearest template, fit positive gain, fit gain and coarse slope grid [-.45,.45] step .15. No unknown rejection, sensor calibration uncertainty, within-element scatter, finite LED bandwidth, detector spectral response, polarization, oxide-film optics, or alloy mixing. No measured data on actual target alloy pairs.
At noise .02 and achromatic gain variation only, raw 3 bands 27.63%, gain fit 3 bands 51.42%, 5 bands 73.38%. At spectral slope SD .05, gain fit 3 bands 48.42%, 5 bands 58.18%. At slope SD .15, 5 bands gain fit 37.57%, gain+slope fit 44.15%. Slope correction discards useful material signal under weaker distortion. Closed-set nominal accuracy, not empirical performance.
## Hardware and next steps
Use shielded head, sequential low-power LEDs, photodiode and dark measurement, calibrated reference for each wavelength and consistent incidence/collection geometry. Compare specular and diffuse channels as surface-state indicators, rotate probe or use multiple angles. Measure actual LED spectral power and detector response, calibrate their convolution. Do not infer alloy composition by linear mixing of elemental optical constants. Test clean, machined, oxidized, oily and coated samples separately; use optical sensor as surface-condition gate for other modalities, not just a grade classifier. Chemical color sorting is a separate, surface-altering modality; use documented commercial/laboratory procedures only. Evaluate incremental benefit with held-out lots.
