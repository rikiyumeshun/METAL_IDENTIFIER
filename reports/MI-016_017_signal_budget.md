# MI-016 / MI-017 — Multi-condition low-cost sensing
## MI-016
2026 study: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12845692/ reports Al alloy conductivity error up to 70.46% for 1.14 mm liftoff change without compensation and <5.57% with multifrequency compensation, using Hioki IM3536 ±0.05% LCR meter. This is not evidence of equivalent cheap hardware performance. Another approach uses loss peak frequency for thick nonmagnetic plates: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0963869523002396 .
Simulation is an intentionally simple single-relaxation model y=g*x/(1+x²)+noise, x=f*pi*mu0*sigma*a²; a=3 mm characteristic scale, not a calibrated coil solution. Gain g=exp(-2*h/3) with h mm uniformly 0.2-1.7. Noise SD in normalized units 0.005/0.02/0.05, NOT % of each signal. Fit unknown positive gain and conductivity by 350-point log grid. Single-frequency is structurally unidentifiable when gain unknown; excluded from accuracy. 8 nominal classes, 200 trials per class, same random draws across 24 conditions (38,400 condition-trials, 9,600 unidentifiable; not 38,400 independent specimens). No material scatter, finite thickness, parasitics, or phase error. 8 frequencies at noise .02 and nominal geometry yield 91.25% closed-set accuracy; +10% effective length scale gives 59.81% and median conductivity error 21.08%. In this model sigma*a² is inseparable: more frequencies cannot remove geometry model bias. Need actual coil forward model and controlled thick flat reference coupons, then geometry/liftoff generalization.
Reproduce with Node.js: node simulation/MI-016_frequency_sweep.mjs . Equivalent JS computation executed during this research run; file execution itself not independently verified.
## MI-017
Published cutting-tool/workpiece calibration https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11740-023-01260-0 eqs 3-4 for cemented carbide paired with EN AW-2017 and EN AW-7075 gives effective slopes 1000/83.56 and 1000/60.98 uV/K (about 11.97 and 16.40). These are effective slopes fitted over 293-730 K, not standalone absolute Seebeck coefficients or verified near-room-temperature slopes. Difference ~4.43 uV/K; illustrative 30 K differential gives ~133 uV. For equal-prior Gaussian binary model with common total voltage SD, 95% ideal classification needs SD <133/3.289707 ~40.4 uV, including material scatter and all instrument errors. This is a screening signal budget only; actual low-temperature slope, reference electrode compatibility, oxide/contact, temperature gradients and offsets must be measured. A second study https://doi.org/10.1088/2053-1591%2Fac2345 cites aging-related 6061 thermopower change <=0.25 uV/K; treat as secondary citation pending primary verification, not classification effect size.
Measurement concept: two contacts with same characterized electrode material and different measured junction temperatures; record zero-gradient offset, heat and cool, fit slope and intercept, reverse gradient where feasible. Use reference coupons and multiple lots. Shared heater, temperature measurement and low-noise voltage front end can also support thermal transient and current-reversed resistance. Do not assume independence of derived features.
