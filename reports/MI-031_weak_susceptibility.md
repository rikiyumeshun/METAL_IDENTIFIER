# MI-031 — Weak magnetic susceptibility and mechanical readout
## Evidence and transcription
NIST report, Table 2, printed p.1194: https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=30485 . Original page image was inspected: C11000 ETP copper room-temperature susceptibility is POSITIVE 3.22E-5, despite a negative sign in the automated PDF text extraction. Independent compilation https://researchmeasurements.com/298-2/ table A6.8b agrees with positive sign. C10100 oxygen-free copper -9.37E-6, C51000 phosphor bronze -5.86E-6, 2024 1.93E-5, 6061 1.90E-5, 7075-T6 1.57E-5, 5083 1.68E-5, pure Al 2.07E-5. 17 selected sample records transcribed. C10100 and C11000 are UNS examples, not exact certified JIS C1020/C1100 equivalence. NIST reports system accuracy 5%, precision 3%, and warns random specimens need not agree within 10% owing to composition and processing; contamination can change magnetic properties. Room-temperature low-field slope measured up to about 4 kA/m; extrapolation to permanent magnet fields is not validated, especially for contaminated samples. Treat copper sign difference as a priority hypothesis, not a universal grade property.
Low-cost force method precedent: Bonin & Bonetto, IEEE TIM 71 (2022), https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/items/eb524785-62fb-4694-b685-374fdc2758b7/full ; uses magnets, balance and whole-sample force calculation.
## Force budget
Linear isotropic weak susceptibility, approximate volume-averaged field gradient: F_z = (chi_sample-chi_air)*V/(2*mu0)*mean(dB²/dz); air chi=.36 ppm assumed. Signed apparent mass F/g. For V=1 cm³, mean gradient 20 T²/m, 1 ppm corresponds to .81146 mg. 153 combinations (17 samples x 3 volumes x 3 gradients). Gradient and effective volume are design assumptions, not demonstrated field/fixture performance. The large 316 susceptibility estimate is particularly provisional; ferromagnetic contamination and nonlinear response can invalidate linear force model.
## Pair sensitivity
Five pairs, 20,000 shared draws each, 27 combinations per pair = 2,700,000 condition-trials. Relative material SD 10/25/50% of absolute nominal chi, independent force readout SD 1/5/10 mg, volume .1/1/5 cm³, gradient fixed 20 T²/m; independent gradient-scale error SD 3%. Class-dependent Gaussian likelihood approximation using known nuisance variance. No systematic offsets, unknown classes, cross-property fusion or real lot distribution. At 1 cm³, material SD 25% and force SD 5 mg, C10100/C11000 99.46%, C10100/C51000 60.91%, 2024/6061 51.38%, 6061/7075 58.96%, 5083/7075 52.61%. Copper-pair nominal force difference 33.73 mg; 2024/6061 .243 mg. Equal-SD 95% theoretical total force SD budget = nominal difference/3.289707; does not include validated material distribution. Copper-pair nominal force difference is intriguing but uncertainty in sign and lot variation cannot be represented reliably by assumed relative Gaussian scatter. No actual grade accuracy claim.
## Validation priorities
Collect multiple independent certified oxygen-free and ETP copper lots, identify exact UNS/JIS grades and processing conditions. Check whether sign persists at multiple fields after accounting for remanence, orientation and ferromagnetic contamination. Use nonmagnetic fixtures, secure magnet and sample, blank tare, reference samples, independent volume measurement and field-map integration. Stop motion and wait for eddy-current forces to decay before static measurement. Use safe fixed magnet spacing and prevent pinch/projectile hazards; strong ferromagnetic samples require a separate low-field screening mode. A force channel can share the load cell with density and magnetic drag, but fixture geometry and measurement ranges differ; balance response to magnet itself must be characterized. Prioritize this pair-specific experiment over adding yet another hypothetical classifier.
